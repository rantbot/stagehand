How it works

Two windows

The slides

The one you share

Just the slides, full screen. No controls, no notes, nothing to hide.

Your script · next slide · timer

The one you keep

Your script, the next slide, a timer, and every control.

Stagehand runs a talk across two windows. The one you share on a call or a projector shows only the slides. The one you keep has your script, a preview of what comes next, a timer, and every button. They stay in step on their own. Click Next in either one and both move. NOTE: This deck is the demo and the template at once. View the source to see how each slide is written.

Writing a deck

A slide is a section

<!-- everything inside the section is the slide -->
<section data-title="Two windows">
  <p class="eyebrow">How it works</p>
  <h2>Two windows</h2>

  <!-- and the aside is what you say -->
  <aside>Stagehand runs a talk across two windows.</aside>
</section>
Each slide is a section in one HTML file. Whatever is inside it is the slide, laid out on a 1920 by 1080 canvas, so it looks the same on a laptop and a projector. The aside is your script. It never shows on the shared screen. It shows, large, in your window. Any line in the aside that starts with a capital label and a colon, like NOTE: or SOURCES:, is kept out of your script. Use those for reminders to yourself.

One thing at a time

Reveals

  1. Add class="reveal" to anything on a slide.

  2. Write (click) in your script where it should appear.

  3. Next reveals them in order, then moves on.

Anything with the reveal class stays hidden until you click. (click) Mark it with class reveal. (click) Then write the word click in brackets in your script, where you want it to appear. In your window that turns into a numbered button, so you can see exactly where you are. (click) Next reveals them in order, and once they are all showing, it moves to the next slide.

Pointing at things

Spotlight

Add only to a reveal and it shows for its own click, then steps aside for the next. Put a ring on it to circle part of a diagram as you talk.

Sometimes you want to point at one part of a picture at a time. (click) Work arrives in the inbox. (click) Someone reviews it. (click) And then it ships. Each ring is a reveal with the only class, so it shows for its own click and steps aside for the next.

Optional

Sound and narration

A bed under a slidedata-sound="room.mp3" A voice over a slidedata-voice="intro.mp3" A voice over the titlethe same, on the title card

Beds crossfade between slides and dip while a voice plays. Volume and mute live in your window.

Sound is optional, and this demo has none. Put data-sound on a slide and a recording loops quietly under it. Slides that share a recording carry it through without a seam. Put data-voice on a slide, or on the title card, and a recording plays once when you arrive, with the room tone dipping underneath. A recorded narrator over the title card makes a strong cold open.

Full screen, mute, open your window

Open the deck and click it once. That turns the sound on and opens your window. Click it once more to go full screen, then share it. From then on, drive everything from your window, or from a clicker. The dots along the bottom jump to any slide. Edit lets you rewrite your script on the spot, and keeps your changes in that browser. If you refresh by accident, it comes back to the same slide.

The quickest way to make your own is to write it in Markdown and drop or paste it on the start page. Each slide picks its own layout, and you choose Classic, Paper or Signal. For more polish, hand the same file to Claude with the Stagehand skill. It tightens the words and moves the rest into your script. And if you want full control, copy the repository and start from blank.html or one of the templates. And tell us what broke, or what you wished it did.
